The short answer

A Google Ads audit checks, in order, whether the account counts real leads or sales, whether its money reaches the right searches, and whether its settings let automated bidding learn. Start with conversion tracking, because every other number depends on it, then work through search terms, match types, bidding, budget, ads, landing pages, location settings, Performance Max and account access.

Time needed: about three to four hours for a typical small to mid-size Malaysian account, using the 11 checks below.

How to use this checklist

How do you audit a Google Ads account?

Work through the checks in order. Each one says what to look at, where it sits in Google Ads, what bad looks like, and how long it takes. Order matters: if check 1 fails, the numbers in checks 2 to 11 are not telling you the truth.

The audit, ticked in sequence

It is the pre-takeover audit from our guide to fixing Google Ads accounts, laid out so you can run it yourself. Or hand it to our Google Ads management team and we run it free.

Part 1 · The data

What should you check first in a Google Ads audit?

The data. Smart bidding chases whatever the account calls a conversion, so a broken definition spoils every later finding.

01

Conversion tracking: is what is counted real?

About 30 to 45 min
Look at
Every conversion action, whether it is Primary or Secondary, and what it fires on. Primary actions fill the Conversions column and drive bidding. Secondary actions are observation only (Source: Google Ads Help, 2026).
Where
Goals menu, then Conversions, then Summary.
Bad looks like
Page views or WhatsApp icon clicks counted as primary conversions next to real leads. Two tags firing for one lead. More conversions than the enquiries your team actually received.

If this fails, fix it before anything else. Our Google Ads conversion tracking guide covers the setup.

02

Account structure and campaign types

About 15 min
Look at
Which campaign types are running, how budget splits between them, and whether brand searches sit apart from generic ones.
Where
The Campaigns table, last 90 days.
Bad looks like
Many small campaigns, each too thin to learn. Brand and generic keywords mixed together, so cheap brand clicks hide an expensive generic campaign.

Part 2 · The waste

How do you find wasted spend in a Google Ads account?

Read what people really searched, then check that keywords and ads are tight enough to earn good Quality Scores.

03

Search terms and negative keywords

About 30 to 60 min
Look at
What people actually typed before clicking, over 90 days, sorted by cost. A keyword is what you bid on. A search term is what they searched (Source: Google Ads Help, 2026).
Where
Campaigns, then Insights and reports, then Search terms.
Bad looks like
Spend on job seekers ("jawatan kosong"), freebie hunters, DIY searches or places you do not serve, in English and in Malay. A negative keyword list nobody has touched for months.
04

Match types, ad relevance and Quality Score

About 20 min
Look at
The mix of broad, phrase and exact match, and Quality Score. Its three components are expected clickthrough rate, ad relevance and landing page experience, each rated Above average, Average or Below average (Source: Google Ads Help, 2026).
Where
Keywords, then the columns icon, then the Quality score section.
Bad looks like
Broad match on manual bidding, when Google says it is critical to use Smart Bidding with broad match. Top spending keywords rated Below average on the same component, which shows you what to fix.

Part 3 · The engine

Are bidding and budget set up so the account can learn?

Automated bidding needs conversions to work with. Budget only helps if you are losing impressions to budget.

05

Bidding strategy versus conversion volume

About 10 min
Look at
Each campaign's bid strategy, set against the conversions it actually records each month.
Where
Each campaign's settings, under Bidding.
Bad looks like
A Target CPA campaign with a handful of conversions a month, or targets edited every few days. Google recommends judging Target CPA over the last 30 days with at least 30 conversions (Source: Google Ads Help, 2026).
06

Budget and impression share

About 10 min
Look at
Why your ads did not show. Search lost IS (budget) and Search lost IS (rank) split the reason, and both are campaign level columns (Source: Google Ads Help, 2026).
Where
Add both as columns in the Campaigns table.
Bad looks like
Your best campaign losing impressions to budget while a weak one has money to spare. Heavy loss to rank, which means Quality Score or bids, so a bigger budget will not fix it.

For realistic RM budgets, see our Google Ads pricing guide and what ads cost in Malaysia.

Part 4 · The offer

Do the ads and landing pages earn the click?

Both feed Quality Score, so both affect what you pay per click.

07

Ads and assets

About 20 min
Look at
Your responsive search ads and the assets around them. Each ad takes up to 15 headlines and 4 descriptions, and Ad strength is rated from Poor to Excellent (Source: Google Ads Help, 2026).
Where
The Ads view and the Assets view.
Bad looks like
One ad per ad group with three headlines. Promotions that ended months ago. An old number in a call asset. No mention of WhatsApp, when that is how most Malaysian customers want to reach you.
08

Landing page and speed

About 15 min
Look at
Where each ad sends people, whether the page answers the search, and how it loads on a phone.
Where
Campaigns, then Insights and reports, then Landing pages. Check the Mobile-friendly click rate column.
Bad looks like
Every ad pointing at the homepage. Slow loading on mobile data. Eight-field forms. Google calls landing page speed one of the easiest ways to improve mobile ad results (Source: Google Ads Help, 2026).

Part 5 · The settings

Which settings quietly leak budget in Malaysia?

Three quick checks that most accounts we review have skipped.

09

Location targeting: Presence or Presence or interest

About 5 min
Look at
"Presence or interest" is the default and reaches people in, regularly in, or who have shown interest in your targeted locations. "Presence" reaches people in or regularly in them (Source: Google Ads Help, 2026).
Where
Each campaign's settings, under Locations.
Bad looks like
A Petaling Jaya clinic left on the default, reaching people outside its area who only searched about it. With Singapore next door and many Malaysians searching from abroad, that traffic is real. Local service businesses usually want Presence. Travel, property and education businesses selling overseas may want the default.
10

Performance Max checks

About 20 to 30 min
Look at
Which searches Performance Max is matching, and whether it is taking your own brand searches.
Where
Campaigns, then Search terms, then "Search terms and landing pages for Performance Max". Negative keywords in Performance Max only apply to Search and Shopping inventory (Source: Google Ads Help, 2026).
Bad looks like
Most conversions coming from your own brand name, with no brand exclusions set. Asset groups with one image and generic text. Nobody checking overlap with Search.

More in our Performance Max guide.

11

Change history, access and ownership

About 10 to 15 min
Look at
What changed recently, who can get into the account, and whose account it really is.
Where
Campaigns menu, then Change history, which covers the past 2 years. Admin menu, then Access and security.
Bad looks like
Performance swings after changes or auto-applied recommendations nobody approved. A former agency or staff member still holding Admin. Only one admin, which Google itself flags as a risk. An account under someone else's login.
