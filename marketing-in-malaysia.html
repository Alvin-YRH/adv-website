Standard vs Malaysia

What changes when you market in Malaysia?

The short answer

Marketing in Malaysia means planning by language, not only by search demand. People search the same need in English, Malay, Chinese and local spellings like "aircond", and each wording has its own volume and cost. So keyword lists, ad copy and landing pages are built per language, and how much each language matters depends on your industry.

Start with keywords and copy. That is where the two playbooks differ most.

Read the detail

Most marketing playbooks are written for one language market. You research keywords, rank them by search volume, write one set of ads and send everyone to the same landing page. In Malaysia that leaves money on the table, because the same need is searched in several languages and spellings, and each one behaves like its own small market.

Keywords and copy. A Malay speaker looking for car insurance types "insurans kereta". A Chinese speaker looking for a dentist types 牙医. Plenty of people type "aircond", the way Malaysians say it, not "aircon". Each wording has its own search volume and its own bid range, so each one needs its own list and its own ads. Translating one English ad is not enough, because people search and decide differently in each language.

Platforms. We match platforms to who you sell to, not one mix for everyone. In the accounts we run, Instagram works well for Chinese-speaking audiences in lead generation, and TikTok works best with Malay-language content. Our Meta ads service plans Facebook and Instagram around that.

Lead capture. For most industries, the lead goes to WhatsApp first. The website still matters, but its role depends on the industry.

Calendar. Ramadan and Hari Raya, Chinese New Year, Deepavali, 11.11 and 12.12 are the major Malaysian sales and festive periods. Plan creative and budget around them instead of a Black Friday and Q4 calendar.

Costs and tax. Use RM benchmarks, not USD ones. Google ad spend for Malaysian business accounts carries 8% SST on top. Our Google Ads pricing guide and advertising costs guide have the RM numbers.

Compliance. Platform policy is only the first check. Malaysian rules apply too. Health service ads need approval from the Medicine Advertisements Board (KKLIU) before they run, under Act 290.

How to read this data

  • Keyword Planner reports total searches in Malaysia for each exact wording. It does not split searches by the searcher's language.
  • Chinese terms are matched in Google's grouped form, so close variants can be counted together.
  • "No data returned" means Google did not report a figure. It does not mean nobody searches.
  • Google rounds small volumes, so treat small numbers as a rough guide.
Read the detail

We ran English, Malay, Chinese and Malaysian spelling versions of the same need through Google Keyword Planner for seven industries. The point was simple: does one English keyword list capture Malaysian demand? In most of these industries it does not.

Malay beats English for car insurance. "insurans kereta" gets 12,100 searches a month against 9,900 for "car insurance". An English only campaign would miss the bigger of the two.

The Malaysian spelling wins for aircon. "aircond service" is searched 6,600 times a month, almost twice as often as "aircon service" at 3,600. Add "servis aircond" at 1,900 and the Malay and local spellings together outweigh the textbook English term.

Chinese demand for dentists is large. 牙医 gets 5,400 searches a month. Its low top of page bid is RM0.98, about half the RM1.91 for "dental clinic".

Malay or Chinese terms usually have the lower bid. In every industry here with more than one priced term, the lowest bid at the low end of the range belongs to a Malay or Chinese term. "pengecas ev" starts at RM0.45 against RM0.76 for "ev charger malaysia". 滤水器 starts at RM0.98 against RM1.31 for "water filter". Bids are not final prices. What you pay depends on competition and the quality of your ads and landing pages.

Some industries really are English led. "ev charger malaysia", "cleaning service" and "water filter" carry most of the demand in their groups. That is the point of checking: you only add languages where the demand is.

The same lists feed your content too. Pages written in the language people search in are also the pages search engines and AI answers can match. Our SEO, AEO and GEO guide covers that side.

Step by step

How do you build keyword lists by language?

Six steps. Keep each language separate from keyword to landing page, then judge it on cost per lead.

01

List the need in every language

Write how people say it in English, Malay and Chinese, plus local spellings like "aircond".

02

Check demand for each wording

Run every version through Google Keyword Planner, set to Malaysia.

03

Group ad groups by language

Keep each language in its own ad group, so keywords, ads and bids stay matched.

04

Write ads in the keyword's language

A Malay search gets a Malay ad. A Chinese search gets a Chinese ad.

05

Send each language to a matching page

A landing page or a WhatsApp message in the same language as the ad.

06

Compare cost per lead by language

Not just cost per click. A cheap click only counts if it turns into a lead.

The last step is the one most people skip. A low bid is only a good deal if the leads come in.

Read the detail

1. List the need in every language. Start with what the customer wants, not with one keyword. Write how people say it in English, Malay and Chinese, then add local spellings. "aircond", "servis aircond" and 冷气维修 are all the same need.

2. Check demand for each wording. Put every version into Google Keyword Planner with the location set to Malaysia. Note the monthly searches and the top of page bid range for each. Drop the ones with no demand, but remember that "no data returned" is not the same as zero.

3. Group ad groups by language. Mixing languages in one ad group means one ad has to serve every searcher. Separate ad groups keep each keyword, ad and bid matched.

4. Write ads in the keyword's language. Write each ad for how that audience searches and decides, not as a word for word translation of the English ad.

5. Send each language to a matching page. A Malay ad that lands on an English page loses people. Use a landing page in the same language, or open a WhatsApp chat with a first message in that language.

6. Compare cost per lead by language. Cheaper bids do not always mean cheaper leads. Track leads by ad group and compare cost per lead, not only cost per click. That is how we run language splits inside our Google Ads management.
