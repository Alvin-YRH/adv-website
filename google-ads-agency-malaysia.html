What we do

What does a Google Ads agency do?

The short answer

A Google Ads agency plans, builds and runs your campaigns on Google, and should be judged on the leads or sales they bring in. At ADV that covers Search, Performance Max, Display, YouTube and Demand Gen, with conversion tracking in GA4 and Google Tag Manager set up first. Then comes the weekly work: cleaning up search terms, checking location settings, adjusting bids and budgets, and reporting cost per lead.

Search

Text ads for people already searching for what you sell. Usually where we start.

Performance Max

One campaign across Search, YouTube, Display, Gmail, Maps and Discover, run by Google's automation.

Display

Image ads on websites and apps in Google's network. Mostly for remarketing to past visitors.

YouTube

Video ads before and during YouTube videos, for reach and for warming up buyers.

Demand Gen

Image and video ads in the YouTube, Discover and Gmail feeds, built to create interest.

Conversion tracking

GA4 and Google Tag Manager set up to count forms, calls and WhatsApp taps.

Search term cleanup

A weekly look at what people really typed, and negative keywords for the waste.

Location settings

Targeting the areas you serve, then checking where the clicks really came from.

Most accounts we take on need tracking and search term cleanup before they need a new campaign.

Read the detail

Search campaigns. Someone in Petaling Jaya types "aircond service near me", a firm in Penang searches for an accountant, and your ad shows at that moment. We build campaigns around the words your buyers use and send people to a page that answers the search.

Performance Max. PMax uses Google's automation to show your ads across all of Google's channels from one campaign. It can work well when tracking is clean and there are enough conversions to learn from. It can also spend on placements you would never pick yourself. We use it where the data supports it, and we check where the money goes. Our Performance Max guide covers when it fits an SME.

Display, YouTube and Demand Gen. These reach people who are not looking for you yet. They cost less per view than Search, and we mostly use them to bring back past visitors, or to build awareness when Search volume is limited. The comparison table below shows where each one fits.

Conversion tracking. This comes before anything else. We set up GA4 and Google Tag Manager so the account counts real leads: a submitted form, a call from the ad, a tap on the WhatsApp button. In Malaysia a lot of enquiries start on WhatsApp, so an untracked WhatsApp tap is a lead Google never learns from. Our conversion tracking guide shows how it is set up.

Search term cleanup. Every week we read what people actually typed before they clicked, and add negative keywords for searches that will never buy. In audits this is often the quickest fix.

Location settings. Google's default targets people in your area or showing interest in it, which can put your ads in front of people far from where you work. We set it to match where your customers really are.

Start with Search if people already look for what you sell. Add the others once tracking is clean and Search is working.

Most SMEs we work with run Search first. Performance Max comes in once there are enough conversions for Google to learn from.

Read the detail

Search fits most service businesses. You pay per click, you choose the words, and you can see which searches brought in leads.

Performance Max hands most of the decisions to Google. With clean conversion tracking and enough data, it can find leads that Search alone misses. Google recommends giving a new PMax campaign at least 6 weeks before you judge it. Read our Performance Max guide for when it fits a Malaysian SME.

Display is cheap per click, but the intent is low. We rarely run Google Display ads as a lead campaign on their own. They earn their place as remarketing, showing your ad again to people who already visited your site.

YouTube suits businesses that need to explain what they do. Skippable ads can be skipped after five seconds, so the opening has to say who the ad is for.

Demand Gen and Shopping are not in the table. Demand Gen behaves like social ads inside Google's feeds. Shopping shows your products with a photo and a price, either in a Shopping campaign or inside Performance Max, and it needs a product feed in Google Merchant Center.

Ad spend goes to Google, inside your own Google Ads account. Google has no minimum; you set a daily budget. For Malaysian business accounts, 8% SST is charged on ad spend, so RM3,000 of ads comes to about RM3,240 with SST.

The management fee goes to ADV for planning, building, running and reporting on the account. The tiers above are a guide, not a quote. Where you land depends on the number of campaigns and the tracking work needed. Above RM20,000 a month in ad spend, the fee is about 15% of ad spend, and for selected clients part of it can be profit sharing.

Some Google advertising packages bundle the ad spend and the fee into one price. That makes it hard to see how much actually reached Google. We keep them as separate numbers, every month.

Minimum term. The minimum engagement is 3 months, because communication and a working rhythm between both sides take time to build. It can vary by client and industry. Johor Bahru. A JB business often has two audiences: people in Johor, and people in Singapore looking for services in JB. Whether to include Singapore is a budget decision we make from the search terms, not a guess.

Penang, Ipoh, Kuching and elsewhere. We set location targeting to the areas you actually serve, and use the location report to see where clicks really came from. For nationwide businesses, we split out cheaper areas once the data shows a difference.

Language. Malaysians search in English, Malay and Chinese, and often mix them. Keywords and ads can be written for each, and the search terms report shows which ones your customers actually use.
