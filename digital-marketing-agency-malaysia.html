Choosing a Digital Marketing Agency in Malaysia

By Alvin Yap, founder of ADV · Updated 26 October 2026

Most business owners pick an agency on the pitch. Pick on the answers instead. Here is what to ask, the red flags, what it costs in RM, and a scorecard to compare agencies side by side.

How to choose

How do you choose a digital marketing agency in Malaysia?

The short answer

To choose a digital marketing agency in Malaysia, ask every agency the same five things before you sign. Who runs your account day to day, can they show results with dates, what exactly the fee covers, who owns the ad accounts and data, and how they report leads. A good agency answers all five plainly and puts them in writing.

  • Who runs your account
  • Results with dates
  • What the fee covers
  • Who owns the accounts
  • How they report

Ask every agency the same questions. Then you are comparing answers, not pitches.

Before you sign

What should you ask a digital marketing agency before you sign?

Eight questions. A good agency answers each one plainly, without needing to go away and check.

01

Who will actually run my account day to day?

Why it matters
The person who pitches is not always the person who does the work. Their experience is what you are paying for.
A good answer sounds like
"This named person runs it. Here is their role, and here is how you reach them directly."
02

Can I see results with dates, including campaigns that did not work?

Why it matters
A result with no date or source cannot be checked. Every agency has misses. The honest ones can explain theirs.
A good answer sounds like
"Here is a result from a stated month, what we changed, and a campaign that did not work and what we did next."
03

What does the fee cover, and what is extra?

Why it matters
Creative, landing pages, tracking setup and reports are sometimes billed on top. You want to know before the first invoice.
A good answer sounds like
"Here is what is included every month, in writing, and the price of anything outside it."
04

Do I pay ad spend directly to Meta and Google, or through you?

Why it matters
If spend goes through the agency, you need to see the platform invoices and know whether there is a markup.
A good answer sounds like
"You pay the platforms from your own payment method. If it ever goes through us, you see the invoices and any markup in writing."
05

Who owns the ad accounts, pixel, tags and data?

Why it matters
If the agency owns them, you can lose your history, audiences and tracking when you leave.
A good answer sounds like
"You do. Everything sits under your business, and we get access to work on it."
06

How do you track leads, and what counts as a conversion?

Why it matters
A page view or a button click is not a lead. If the counting is wrong, the bidding and the reports are wrong too.
A good answer sounds like
"We count real enquiries, like a submitted form or a call, and check the numbers against what your team actually received."
07

How often do you report, and what is in it?

Why it matters
A report full of clicks and impressions can hide the fact that no leads came in.
A good answer sounds like
"On a set rhythm. Spend, leads, cost per lead, what changed and what we will do next."
08

What is the minimum term, and why?

Why it matters
A minimum term can be fair. It is fair when there is a reason for it and a clear way out.
A good answer sounds like
"This is the minimum, this is why, and this is the notice you give if you want to leave."

Ask for the answers to 3, 4 and 5 in writing. They are the ones that cost you money if they are vague.

Read the detail

Who runs your account. In a pitch you usually meet the most senior person in the room. Ask who will open your account on a normal Tuesday, make the changes and answer your messages. Then ask to speak to that person before you sign.

Results with dates. "We grew leads by a lot" means nothing without a month, a channel and a source. Ask for a dated example, and ask about a campaign that did not work. An agency that says it has never had a miss is either very new or not telling you everything.

What the fee covers. Get the list in writing: campaign management, creative, landing pages, tracking setup, reporting, calls. Anything outside the list should have its own price, so there are no surprises in month two.

How ad spend is paid. The cleanest setup is that you pay Meta and Google directly, from your own payment method, inside your own ad accounts. If spend goes through the agency, ask for the platform invoices every month and for any markup to be stated in writing.

Who owns the accounts. The ad accounts, the Meta pixel, Google Tag Manager, Google Analytics and the data in them should belong to your business. The agency works on them with access you give and can take back.

How leads are tracked. Ask what fires a conversion. A submitted form, a call or a booked appointment is a lead. A page view is not. Then ask whether they check the platform numbers against the enquiries your team actually received. Our Google Ads audit checklist shows what to look for.

Reporting. Agree on how often, and on what is in it. Spend, leads and cost per lead come first. If you sell online, add sales and revenue. A good report also says what changes next and why.

Minimum term. Paid ads need time to learn and a working rhythm to build, so a minimum term is not a red flag on its own. What matters is that the agency tells you the reason, and that the exit terms are written down.

Red flags

What are the red flags when choosing a digital marketing agency?

Seven warning signs. Any one of them is worth a direct question before you sign.

01Red flag

Guaranteed results, or "number 1 on Google"

No agency controls the ad auction or the search rankings. Google's own guide for people hiring an SEO says "No one can guarantee a #1 ranking on Google."

Source: Google Search Central, "Do you need an SEO?"

AskWhat exactly is guaranteed, and what happens if it is missed?

02Red flag

The agency owns your ad account, or will not give you access

If you cannot log in to your own account, you cannot check the work, and you may lose your history when you leave.

AskWhose business is the ad account under, and can I have admin access today?

03Red flag

Results with no dates or no source

A screenshot of one good month says nothing about the other months, or where the number came from.

AskWhen was this, which platform, and can you show me where the number comes from?

04Red flag

Reports with clicks and impressions, never leads or sales

Clicks are easy to grow. Leads and sales are what you pay for.

AskHow many leads came in last month, and what did each one cost?

05Red flag

Fees that bundle or hide ad spend

A single package price makes it hard to know how much actually reached Meta or Google.

AskOf this amount, how much is ad spend and how much is your fee?

06Red flag

A long lock in with no exit terms, or no reason given

A minimum term with a reason is normal. A long one with no reason and no way out is not.

AskWhy this length, and what do I need to do to leave?

07Ask about it

One person handling too many accounts

You cannot tell how much attention your account will get unless you ask.

AskHow many accounts does the person on mine handle right now?

Read the detail

Guarantees. Google's Search Central page for people hiring an SEO is direct about this: no one can guarantee a top ranking, and it tells you to be wary of anyone who claims to. Paid ads work the same way. Costs and results move with competition, your offer and your landing page. An agency can promise effort, a process and honest reporting. It cannot promise the outcome.

Account ownership. Your ad accounts, pixel, tags and analytics should sit under your business. If an agency runs your ads inside its own account, you cannot see everything, and you may not be able to take your history and audiences with you.

Undated results. Ask for the month, the channel and the source. A dated result can be checked. An undated one cannot.

Reports without leads. Clicks and impressions are useful context. They are not the result. If a report never shows leads or sales, ask why.

Bundled fees. When the fee and the ad spend are one number, you cannot tell how much reached the platforms. Ask for them separately, every month.

Lock ins. A minimum term is fine when the agency explains it. Ask for the reason and the exit terms in writing.

Workload. There is no single right number of accounts per person. It depends on the size of the accounts and the support around them. That is why it is worth asking.

Agency types

Big agency, boutique agency or freelancer?

Each one fits a different business. Choose on who does the work and how you want to work with them.

ADV is a boutique, so read the boutique column with that in mind. The questions above work for all three.

Read the detail

Big agencies bring range. If you need TV, outdoor, a brand campaign and media buying under one contract, they can do it. The trade off is layers. The senior team may set the strategy while a junior executive runs the account each day, and smaller clients can get less attention than the large ones.

Boutique agencies tend to focus on a few channels, and the person who sells the work often does the work. That suits SMEs who want to talk directly to whoever runs their ads. The trade off is capacity. A small team can only take on so many accounts, and it may not have a specialist for every channel.

Freelancers are usually the cheapest and the most direct. They suit a clear, contained job, like setting up tracking or running one campaign. The risk is cover. If they get busy, fall sick or take a full time job, the work stops.

Whichever you pick, use the same eight questions and the scorecard below. The answers matter more than the size of the office.

Read the detail

Use the same scorecard for every agency you talk to, and score on what they actually said, not on how the pitch felt. If an answer was vague, give it a 1 at most. If they could not answer, it is a 0.

Founder or senior person. Score 2 only if you know who will run the account and can reach them directly.

Dated proof. Score 2 when the results have months and sources, and the agency shows you something that did not work.

Clear fees. Score 2 when the management fee, the extras and the ad spend are separate numbers in writing.

You own the accounts. Score 2 when the ad accounts, pixel, tags and analytics sit under your business.

Tracking. Score 2 when they count real leads and check them against what your team received.

Reporting. Score 2 for a set rhythm that shows leads, cost per lead and what happens next.

Term. Score 2 when the minimum term comes with a reason and written exit terms.

Industry fit. Score 2 when they know your industry, including its rules. Health ads in Malaysia, for example, need approval from the Medicine Advertisements Board (KKLIU) before they run.
